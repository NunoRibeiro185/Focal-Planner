## Design Context

### Users

Wedding and portrait photographers who care deeply about their craft. Two primary contexts:

- **Web (desktop/laptop):** Deep work sessions — setting up packages, reviewing finances, scheduling, managing clients. They have time to explore and configure.
- **Mobile:** Quick checks and updates on the go — glancing at the next booking, marking a task done, checking payment status while at a shoot. Fast and frictionless.

Some photographers will live on mobile. Some on desktop. Both are first-class.

**Emotional context:** These users chose photography as a career because they value beauty and authenticity. They're likely using cold, Excel-like tools right now and feeling like those tools don't represent who they are. Focal should feel like it was built by a photographer, for photographers.

### Brand Personality

**Three words:** personal, passionate, crafted

Focal should feel warm, unhurried, and intentional — like a well-made physical object, not a dashboard. Not a startup product trying to look impressive. Not a legacy enterprise tool trying to look modern. Something in between: a tool with taste.

The emotional goal is quiet confidence. A photographer should open Focal and feel like their business is *seen* and *organized*, not processed and optimized. The interface earns trust through restraint, not features.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Primary theme:** Light. Warm stone/amber tones — the existing OKLCH palette (amber-tinted neutrals, ochre primary) is correct and should be preserved. Not white-white, not cream — the specific warmth of photographic paper.

**Visual references:**
- [meiwensee.com/photography](https://meiwensee.com/photography) — editorial whitespace, photography-first, design recedes
- [margaretrajic.com](https://margaretrajic.com) — generous negative space, sophisticated simplicity, numbered rhythm
- Mel Volkman — intentional, unhurried, every element earns its place

**What this is NOT:**
- Corporate SaaS (cold grays, dense data tables, blue primary buttons everywhere)
- Complex Excel-style management UI
- Generic "startup clean" with Inter and blue gradients
- Dark mode with glowing cyan accents
- Rounded cards with drop shadows stacked in identical grids

**Typography:** Current pairing is Playfair Display (headings) + Montserrat (body). The user has confirmed satisfaction with this pairing — it reads as warm and editorial. Future font explorations should look toward type families with craft/editorial heritage: slab serifs, humanist display faces, or refined grotesques. Avoid the reflex list entirely.

**Color philosophy:** The existing warm OKLCH palette is right. Stone-tinted neutrals, amber-chroma primary. Tint every neutral toward the brand hue. Reserve the amber accent for moments that matter. Don't fight the warmth — lean into it. The palette should feel like natural light through a window, not LED studio lighting.

**Layout philosophy:** Generous whitespace. Asymmetric when possible. Type hierarchy does the heavy lifting — not icons, not cards. Not everything needs a container. Let content breathe. Think editorial spread over dashboard grid.

### Design Principles

1. **Photography-first restraint** — The UI should recede and support the work, never compete with it. Design exists to make the content legible and beautiful, not to demonstrate design effort.

2. **Warmth through specificity** — Generic warmth (rounded corners, friendly icons, pastel colors) reads as corporate-friendly. Real warmth comes from specific choices: the exact tone of a color, the weight of a type pairing, the breathing room around a heading. Every decision should feel made, not defaulted.

3. **Both contexts, no compromises** — Desktop sessions and mobile quick-checks are equally important. Don't design desktop and then shrink it. Design for each context's actual use — depth for desktop, speed for mobile.

4. **Personal scale** — Focal isn't enterprise software. It's a tool a photographer might have built for themselves and shared with friends. Small gestures (a thoughtful empty state, a date formatted just right, a label that sounds human) matter more than feature density.

5. **Trust through restraint** — A financial and scheduling tool earns trust by being clear, not clever. Never sacrifice legibility for visual interest. When in doubt, remove.
