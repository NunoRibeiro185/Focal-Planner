Client Portal — Which model fits best?

Three different ways to think about the client-facing space. Pick the one that feels closest to what you're imagining.

A

Deal Room (per-booking page)

Each booking gets a unique secret link the photographer sends to the client. The client opens it — no account needed — and sees their inquiry, the proposal, and can accept or counter-offer. Like the 2nd shooter view but for clients. Simple, familiar pattern.

Pros

  • No auth complexity
  • Follows existing pattern
  • Photographer controls when to share

Cons

  • One link per booking, no history
  • Client can't revisit past bookings easily
B

Client Account (magic-link login)

When a client submits an inquiry, they get a magic link to a lightweight personal dashboard. They log in via email and can see all their interactions with this photographer — inquiries, proposals, contracts — across multiple bookings over time.

Pros

  • Persistent history
  • More "professional" product feel
  • Multi-booking support

Cons

  • Client must authenticate
  • More infrastructure to build
  • Most clients only book once
C

Shared Timeline (conversation thread)

A shared, scrollable timeline attached to a booking. Both the photographer and client post to it — proposals, messages, contract links, updates. Client accesses via a token link (no account). Think of it like a lightweight shared inbox or Basecamp-style message board per booking.

Pros

  • Very flexible — anything can be a "message"
  • Natural for back-and-forth negotiation
  • Extensible for contracts, invoices later

Cons

  • Less structured than a formal proposal flow
  • More complex to build the right UX
